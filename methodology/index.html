Methodology

This page explains the formula behind each of the site’s seven tools and where their data comes from. Every calculation runs in your browser. The results are estimates for general information.

Data sources

Price levels. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) publishes Regional Price Parities (RPPs). BEA describes them as measures of how price levels differ across states and metropolitan areas in a given year, expressed as a percentage of the overall national price level (U.S. = 100). We use the 2024 RPPs, which BEA released on February 19, 2026, for all 50 states, the District of Columbia and 387 metro areas. BEA publishes an all-items level plus levels for goods, housing (rents), utilities and other services; the tools use all five. Per BEA’s technical notes, RPPs are built from Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI price quotes and Census Bureau American Community Survey rent data. The data covers states and metro areas only, not ZIP codes or neighborhoods.

Fuel price. The moving calculator’s default fuel price is the U.S. average retail price of regular gasoline from the EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update: $4.319 a gallon for the week of September 14, 2026, released September 15, 2026.

Refresh schedule. BEA updates RPPs once a year; its page lists the next release as December 10, 2026, and we update the price levels after each release. EIA publishes gasoline prices weekly, and we refresh the fuel default each week.

Cost of living calculator and salary comparison

Equivalent salary = your salary × the destination’s all-items RPP ÷ your current location’s all-items RPP. The overall difference is (destination RPP ÷ origin RPP − 1) × 100, and the housing, goods, utilities and other services lines use the same ratio with each category’s level. Example: $60,000 in Alabama (88.823) is equivalent to $74,305 in a place priced at 110.

Rent affordability calculator

The tool runs two tests and shows the lower result. Income test: yearly gross income ÷ 12 × your chosen share (30% by default). Landlord test: yearly gross income ÷ the landlord’s multiple (40× monthly rent by default). Example: $90,000 at 25% gives $1,875; at 40× it gives $2,250; the rent that meets both is $1,875. Both percentages are guidelines you can change.

Prorated rent calculator

Prorated rent = daily rate × days charged. Moving in counts the move-in day through the last day of the month; moving out counts the 1st through the date you enter. The daily rate follows one of three methods:

  • Actual days: monthly rent ÷ days in that month.
  • 365-day year: monthly rent × 12 ÷ 365.
  • 30-day month: monthly rent ÷ 30.

Example: $1,500 rent, moving in September 16, 2026, gives 15 days and $750 with actual days, or $739.73 with the 365-day method.

Rent split calculator

  • Equal: rent ÷ number of roommates.
  • By income: rent × each person’s income ÷ everyone’s combined income.
  • By room size: a shared-space percentage of the rent is split equally, and the rest is split by each bedroom’s share of the total bedroom area.

Examples: $3,000 split by incomes of $60,000 and $40,000 gives $1,800 and $1,200. Split by rooms of 200 and 100 square feet with 50% shared, it gives $1,750 and $1,250.

Rent vs buy calculator

The mortgage payment is the standard fixed-rate formula. The tool then runs month by month over your stay. Monthly owning cost = payment + property tax + insurance + maintenance + HOA dues; property tax and maintenance grow with the home’s value each year. Rent grows by your yearly rent increase. Each month, whoever spends less invests the difference at your return rate, and both balances earn that return monthly. The renter starts with the down payment and closing costs invested. At the end, the buyer's wealth is the home’s grown value minus selling costs and the remaining loan, plus the buyer’s investments. The break-even year is the first year, from 1 to 30, in which the buyer’s wealth is at least the renter’s.

Example: a $400,000 home, 20% down, a 6.5% 30-year loan, 1.1% property tax, $1,800 insurance, 1% maintenance, 3% closing and 6% selling costs, 3% home price growth, $2,200 rent rising 3% a year, and a 5% return over seven years. The payment is $2,022.62, the buyer ends with $173,100.59 and the renter with $184,667.94, so renting is ahead by $11,567.35. Buying pays off in year 9. Income taxes, PMI and moving costs are not included.

Moving cost calculator

Total cash = moving cost + packing supplies + travel (hotel + travel days × food per day) + new-home costs (deposit + first month’s rent + anything else). For a truck, moving cost = daily rate × rental days + mileage fee × miles + miles ÷ MPG × fuel price + paid helpers. For movers, moving cost = quote + quote × tip percentage.

Examples: a 500-mile truck move ($40 a day for 3 days, $0.99 a mile, 10 MPG, $4.319 fuel) has $215.95 of fuel and needs $4,450.95 in total, including a $1,500 deposit and $1,500 first rent. With movers, a $2,800 quote plus a 10% tip brings the total to $6,500.

Budget calculator (50/30/20)

Each category = monthly take-home pay × its percentage; weekly amounts = monthly × 12 ÷ 52. The 50/30/20 split is a starting guideline, and the tool warns you if your percentages don’t add up to 100%. Example: $5,000 splits into $2,500, $1,500 and $1,000.

Limits

  • RPPs are averages for a whole state or metro area. Your neighborhood, home and household can differ.
  • Taxes are not included in any cost-of-living comparison.
  • RPPs compare places within one year (2024); they are not a measure of inflation over time.
  • Rules of thumb and rent-vs-buy growth rates are assumptions you set, not forecasts.
  • Mover, truck and storage prices vary by company and date. Use your own written quotes.
