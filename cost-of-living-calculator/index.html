Cost of Living Calculator

A cost of living calculator turns your salary into the pay that buys the same things somewhere else. This one uses the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parities for 2024, released February 19, 2026. By those figures, $60,000 in Alabama has the same buying power as about $74,791 in California.

How does the cost of living calculator work?

The calculator divides the price level of the place you are considering by the price level where you live now, then multiplies your salary by that ratio. The answer is the income that keeps your buying power the same, based on 2024 average prices for each state or metro area.

Equivalent salary = your salary × (new place’s price level ÷ current place’s price level)

You choose two places from one list: all 50 states, the District of Columbia and the 387 metro areas in the tool. Under the headline figure, the tool runs the same ratio for the four categories BEA reports separately: goods, housing (rents), utilities and other services. That breakdown shows which part of a budget creates the gap.

The comparison works at state and metro level only, because that is how the BEA data behind it is published. It does not estimate prices for a ZIP code or a neighborhood. To scan every state at once, open the cost of living by state table.

What are Regional Price Parities?

Regional Price Parities (RPPs) are the Bureau of Economic Analysis’s measure of how price levels differ between places in the same year. Each place is shown as a percentage of the national average, which is set to 100. A state at 110 is about 10% pricier than the country overall; a state at 90 is about 10% cheaper.

BEA says the series lets you compare buying power across the states, or from one metro area to another, “for a given year.” The latest release came out on February 19, 2026 and covers 2024. BEA lists December 10, 2026 as the next release date.

Because RPPs compare places at one point in time, they do not tell you how fast prices are rising. They answer a different question: how far the same paycheck goes in two places.

Worked example: moving from Alabama to California

Someone earning $60,000 in Alabama would need about $74,791 in California to keep the same buying power, which is 24.65% more. The math uses BEA’s 2024 all-items levels, 88.823 for Alabama and 110.72 for California: $60,000 × 110.72 ÷ 88.823.

The reverse move works the same way. $60,000 in California buys about as much as $48,134 in Alabama ($60,000 × 88.823 ÷ 110.72).

Both results describe statewide averages. A move from a small Alabama town to San Francisco, or from Los Angeles to Birmingham, can show a different gap. Pick the metro areas instead of the states in the tool for a closer match.

Why housing drives most of the difference

Rents vary far more from place to place than the prices of goods, so housing explains most of the spread between expensive and cheap areas. In BEA’s 2024 state figures, housing runs from 54.241 in West Virginia to 154.984 in the District of Columbia, while goods stay between 93.597 and 111.562.

Utilities also vary widely, from 70.444 in Idaho to 190.178 in Hawaii.

In the Alabama to California example, goods cost about 10% more while rents cost about 150% more. If rent takes a large share of your spending, your own gap may be bigger than the all-items figure. If you own your home outright, it may be smaller.

What the comparison leaves out

The comparison covers prices only. It does not adjust for income taxes, the cost of the move itself, or what your job pays in the new area. It also averages whole states and metro areas, so a given neighborhood can sit well above or below its regional level.

  • Income taxes: the tool does not add state or local income tax, which differs from state to state.
  • Moving costs: trucks, movers, deposits and first month’s rent are a one-time cost. The moving cost calculator adds them up.
  • Timing: the figures describe 2024. Rents and prices in either place may have moved since.

Treat the result as a starting estimate, then check it against real rent listings and quotes in the place you are considering. How the data is loaded and refreshed is explained on the methodology page.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my cost of living?

Add up what you spend in a typical month on housing, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, debt payments and everything else, then multiply by 12. To estimate the same lifestyle in another place, scale that total by the ratio of the two places’ price levels, which is what this calculator does with your salary. Sorting spending into needs, wants and savings first makes the total easier to trust.

Is $2000 a month a livable wage?

For a single adult, $2,000 a month ($24,000 a year) falls short even in a low-cost state by one widely used measure. The MIT Living Wage Calculator, updated February 15, 2026, puts the pre-tax income a single adult with no children needs in Alabama at $43,814 a year, or $21.06 an hour. Alabama’s 2024 price level, 88.823, is among the lowest in the country.

How much is a 3% cost of living raise?

A 3% raise equals your current salary times 0.03. On $60,000, that is $1,800 more a year, or $150 a month before taxes. For comparison, Social Security benefits rose 2.8% for 2026. A raise only keeps up with your area if local prices rise by less than the raise over the same period.

Can I compare the cost of living by ZIP code?

Not with this tool. It compares states and metro areas, because the BEA price levels it relies on are published at those levels. A metro figure is the closest match for a city and its suburbs. Rents inside one metro can still vary a lot by neighborhood, so check local listings before you decide.
