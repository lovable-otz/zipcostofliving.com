Salary Comparison by City

A salary comparison by city shows what your pay is worth once local prices are counted. Using BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities, $60,000 in the New York-Newark-Jersey City metro area buys about as much as $52,273 in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos, where prices average 12.88% lower.

How much do I need to earn in another city?

Multiply your salary by the new metro area’s price level, then divide by your current metro area’s level. The result is the pay that keeps your buying power the same. The tool opens on New York-Newark-Jersey City (112.563) and Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos (98.066), both from BEA’s 2024 figures.

From New York to Austin: $60,000 × 98.066 ÷ 112.563 = about $52,273. From Austin to New York: $60,000 × 112.563 ÷ 98.066 = about $68,870, or 14.78% more. The two percentages differ because each is measured from a different starting point.

Housing explains much of the gap. Austin’s rent level, 120.361, sits 19.01% below New York’s 148.616. Each metro area includes its suburbs, so the tool compares regions, not ZIP codes or neighborhoods. For whole states, use the cost of living calculator.

Which metro areas are most and least expensive?

In BEA’s 2024 metro data, San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is the most expensive metro area at 115.613, ahead of Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach and Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim. Monroe, Louisiana is the least expensive at 83.597. The last column shows the pay that matches $60,000 at U.S. average prices.

The cheapest areas are smaller metros where rents are low. Monroe’s housing level is 42.815, compared with 194.718 in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont.

Should a job offer be adjusted for cost of living?

It makes sense to weigh local prices when you judge an offer, but price levels are only one input. Employers set pay in their own ways, and an offer in a new city may not move in step with the price difference. Use the adjusted salary as a benchmark for comparison, not as a guaranteed number.

Start by comparing the offer with the salary this tool returns. Suppose you earn $60,000 in New York and receive a $55,000 offer in Austin. That offer is above the $52,273 that matches your New York buying power at 2024 average prices, so on prices alone it goes further.

Then consider what the price index skips: income taxes, commuting, benefits, your actual rent and the one-time cost of relocating. The moving cost calculator totals the cash a move takes, which can help if you are weighing a relocation package.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to compare the cost of living between different cities?

Use one consistent, official price index for both places, then check the costs that matter most to you. BEA’s Regional Price Parities put every state and metro area on the same scale, where the U.S. average equals 100, so any two places compare directly. After that, look at real rent listings, since housing drives most of the gap.

What is a typical cost of living raise for 2026?

There is no single figure for private employers, but one public benchmark is Social Security: benefits increased 2.8% for 2026, as the agency reported in October 2025. Employer raises follow their own budgets and are not tied to that number. On a $60,000 salary, a 2.8% increase would add $1,680 a year.

Can a single person live on $3,000 a month?

It depends on where you live and whether $3,000 is before or after tax. As pre-tax pay, $3,000 a month ($36,000 a year) is below the $43,814 that the MIT Living Wage Calculator says a single adult needs in Alabama, one of the cheaper states (updated February 15, 2026). As take-home pay it goes further, so test it against your own budget.

What are the 50 states ranked by cost of living?

California ranks first at 110.72 in BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities, followed by Hawaii and the District of Columbia. Arkansas ranks last at 86.937, just below Mississippi at 86.953. The full ranking of all 50 states and DC, with housing, goods and utilities broken out, is on the cost of living by state page.

What are the top 10 cities for cost of living in the United States?

By BEA’s 2024 price levels, the ten most expensive metro areas are San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach, Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, New York-Newark-Jersey City, Napa, San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad, Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue, Urban Honolulu, Oxnard-Thousand Oaks-Ventura and San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara. Six of the ten are in California.
