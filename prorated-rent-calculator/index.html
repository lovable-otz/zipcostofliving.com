Prorated Rent Calculator

How do you calculate prorated rent?

Work out a daily rate, then multiply it by the days you occupy the unit. For a move-in, count from the move-in day through the last day of the month. For a move-out, count from the 1st through the last day you pay for. The daily rate depends on the method your lease uses.

Move out October 10, 202610 of 31$483.87$493.15$500.00

The daily rates behind the table:

  • Actual days: $1,500 ÷ days in that month, so $50.00 in September, $51.72 in February 2028 and $48.39 in October.
  • 365-day year: $1,500 × 12 ÷ 365 = $49.32 in every month.
  • 30-day month: $1,500 ÷ 30 = $50.00 in every month.

February shows why the method matters. The same 20 days cost $1,034.48 by actual days or $986.30 by the yearly rate, a difference of $48.18.

Which proration method does my lease use?

Look for the rent or proration clause in your lease; it should say how a partial month is charged. If it names no method, ask the landlord in writing before you pay, and ask for the daily rate and number of days used so you can check the figure with this calculator.

How the three methods compare:

  • Actual days in the month. The daily rate changes with the month’s length. It costs the most per day in February and the least in 31-day months.
  • Daily rate from a 365-day year. A year’s rent divided by 365 gives one rate for every month. It charges less than actual days in 30-day months and February, and more in 31-day months.
  • 30-day month. Rent divided by 30, whatever the calendar says. It charges more than actual days in 31-day months and less in February.

None of these is more correct than the others; each is plain arithmetic. What matters is using the one your lease names, which you can pick from the tool’s drop-down menu.

Is prorated rent required?

Whether a landlord must prorate depends on your lease and the law where you live, so the lease is the first place to look. If it is unclear, your state or local housing agency, or a tenant rights office, can explain the rules that apply to your home.

Some situations are covered by law. California’s official tenant guide, published by the Department of Real Estate (2025 edition), explains the rule for a servicemember who ends a monthly lease under the military termination rules. Termination takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date, and rent is paid on a prorated basis up to that date. The landlord must refund rent paid in advance for later periods within 30 days.

Outside rules like that, a landlord who does not want to prorate may offer a lease that starts on the 1st instead. Compare that offer with the prorated amount before you agree. If you share the unit, the rent split calculator divides the partial month between roommates, and the moving cost calculator totals the rest of your move-in cash.

Frequently asked questions

Is prorated rent divided by 30 or 31?

It depends on the method in your lease. With actual days, rent is divided by the length of that month, so 31 in October and 30 in September. With the 30-day method, it is always divided by 30. A third method divides a year’s rent by 365. At $1,500, ten days in October cost $483.87, $500.00 or $493.15 under those three methods.

How do I calculate prorated rent when I move out?

Count the days from the 1st of the month through your last paid day, then multiply by the daily rate. Moving out on October 10, 2026 with $1,500 rent means 10 of October’s 31 days, or $483.87 by actual days. Confirm which date your lease or notice ends on, since that date sets the count.

What is a 30 day month pro ration?

It treats every month as 30 days long, so the daily rate is always the monthly rent divided by 30. At $1,500, that is $50 a day in any month. It is simple to apply, but it charges a little more than actual days in 31-day months and less in February: 20 days in February 2028 come to $1,000 instead of $1,034.48.

Is prorated rent good or bad?

For a tenant it is usually a fair deal, because you pay only for days you can live in the unit instead of a full month. The catch is timing: a mid-month move-in can mean paying a partial month and the next full month close together. Check when each payment is due so you can plan the cash.
