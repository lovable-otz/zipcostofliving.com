Rent Affordability Calculator

How much rent can I afford? A widely used guideline is 30% of gross monthly income, which is $1,800 a month on $72,000 a year. The Census Bureau (2024) counts households paying more than 30% of income on housing as cost-burdened. This calculator also applies a 40× landlord income test.

How much rent can I afford?

By the 30% guideline, your rent ceiling is your yearly gross income divided by 12, times 0.30. On $72,000 that is $1,800 a month. The calculator also runs a landlord income test and shows the lower of the two results, because you need to pass both to get the lease.

At the default settings both columns match, because 30% of one month’s income equals one-fortieth of a year’s income. Change either setting and they split. At 25% and 40×, $90,000 supports $1,875 by the percentage test and $2,250 by the landlord test, so the tool shows $1,875.

Both tests use pre-tax income. Utilities, renter’s insurance and parking come on top of rent, so a lower percentage leaves more room for them.

Where does the 30% rule come from?

The 30% figure comes from federal housing policy. The Brooke Amendment to the Housing and Urban Development Act of 1969 capped public housing rent at 25% of household income. Laws passed in 1981 and 1983 raised the share tenants in federal rental assistance pay to 30% of adjusted income, according to a Congressional Research Service report dated May 13, 2026.

The same line is now used to measure affordability. The Census Bureau counts households as cost-burdened when housing costs take more than 30% of income, and severely cost-burdened above 50%. For renters, CRS explains, housing cost means gross rent: contract rent plus utilities and fuels. By that measure, 49.4% of renter households were cost-burdened in 2024.

CRS also lists the ratio’s limits. It ignores choices such as paying more to live near work, and it does not reflect how much each household must spend on things like child care, transportation and health care. That is why this tool lets you change the percentage.

What is the 40× rent rule?

The 40× rule is a screening test some landlords use: your yearly gross income should be at least 40 times the monthly rent. For a $2,000 apartment, that means $80,000 a year. It is a landlord practice rather than a law, and each building can set its own number.

Some landlords state the same idea as monthly income of three times the rent. That works out to 36 times the rent per year, a slightly lower bar: $2,000 rent would need $72,000 a year instead of $80,000.

The calculator applies the multiple to the combined income of everyone on the lease. Buildings differ on how they count roommates and whether they accept a guarantor, so ask before you apply. Then set the multiple in the tool to match the listing you are considering.

What if rent is more than 30% of my income?

You would be in common company: about half of renter households paid more than 30% in 2024, per CRS. A higher share leaves less room for everything else, so it helps to map the rest of your budget before signing. Paying more than 50% is the federal definition of a severe cost burden.

Ways to bring the share down:

  • Share the place. The rent split calculator divides one rent by income or room size.
  • Look at cheaper areas. The cost of living calculator compares price levels between states and metro areas.
  • Trim other needs. The budget calculator shows how rent fits into a 50/30/20 split.

In some places a high share is hard to avoid. BEA’s 2024 housing price level for the District of Columbia is 154.984, against 54.241 in West Virginia.

Frequently asked questions

How much rent can I afford on $70,000 a year?

About $1,750 a month under the 30% guideline: $70,000 ÷ 12 × 0.30. A landlord using the 40× income test would also approve rent up to $1,750, because $70,000 ÷ 40 gives the same figure. Utilities and renter’s insurance are extra, so consider leaving room for them.

Can I afford $1000 rent making $20 an hour?

Probably, if you work full time. At 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, $20 an hour comes to $41,600 a year before tax. A $1,000 rent is 28.8% of that monthly income, just under the 30% guideline, and a 40× landlord test needs only $40,000 a year. Fewer hours or unpaid weeks change the answer.

How much rent can I afford if I make $100,000?

Up to $2,500 a month under both the 30% guideline and a 40× landlord test. If a building instead asks for monthly income of three times the rent, $100,000 qualifies for up to about $2,778. These figures use gross income, not take-home pay, and utilities come on top.

Is 40% of monthly income too much for rent?

It is above the federal benchmark: the Census Bureau counts households paying more than 30% of income for housing as cost-burdened, though 40% is under the 50% line for a severe burden. On $70,000 a year, 40% is about $2,333 a month. Whether it works depends on your other costs and savings goals.
