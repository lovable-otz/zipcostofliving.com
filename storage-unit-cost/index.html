How Much Does a Storage Unit Cost?

The average storage unit cost in the U.S. was $89.09 a month in May 2026, across all sizes and types, according to SpareFoot (May 29, 2026). Small 5×5 units averaged $34.89 and 10×20 units $130.14, and your price will depend on size, location and climate control.

How much does a storage unit cost per month?

Most units rent for somewhere between about $35 and $130 a month on average, depending on size. SpareFoot (May 29, 2026) reports these 2026 year-to-date averages through May, based on units rented through its marketplace. Larger units cost more per month but less per square foot.

Location matters too. SpareFoot’s May 2026 state averages ran from $55.83 a month in Oklahoma to $133.08 in Hawaii. It also says prices usually rise in spring, peak by late summer, and are lowest from November to March. Insurance is extra: SpareFoot estimates $10 to $30 a month for $2,000 to $5,000 of coverage.

If storage is part of a move, add it to the “anything else” line in the moving cost calculator.

What size storage unit do I need?

A 5×10 usually holds a one-bedroom apartment, a 10×10 about two bedrooms plus a living room, and a 10×20 a three- or four-bedroom home. That is SpareFoot’s size guide (updated January 12, 2026), which also notes most units are about 8 feet tall, so stacking helps.

HomeSuggested unit
1-bedroom apartment5×10 or 10×10
2-bedroom home10×10 or 10×15
3-bedroom home10×15 or 10×20
4-bedroom home10×20 or 10×30

Two quick checks from the same guide. First, your moving truck hints at the unit size: a 15-foot truck load fits a 10×10, a 20-foot load a 10×15, and a 25-foot load a 10×20. Second, if you are unsure, ask what the next size up costs. A slightly larger unit leaves a path to the back, so you can reach things without unloading everything. If movers will load the unit, their pricing is covered in how much movers cost.

Is climate-controlled storage worth it?

It is worth considering if you are storing things that heat, cold or humidity can damage, or storing for a long time. SpareFoot (May 29, 2026) says climate control can add 10% to 25% to the price of a standard unit. For boxes of sturdy household items over a short move, a standard unit is often enough.

The premium can be modest. In a separate report, SpareFoot (June 26, 2026) put the national average for a climate-controlled unit at $82.39 a month, and the average 10×10 at $74.98 as of February 2026, its lowest in five years.

SpareFoot suggests budgeting for climate control if you are storing items such as:

  • Furniture and mattresses
  • Electronics and appliances
  • Documents, photographs, books and artwork
  • Musical instruments, antiques and collectibles

Think about how much it would cost to replace what you store, then compare that with the extra monthly cost over the months you expect to rent.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a storage unit per month?

About $89 on average. SpareFoot put the U.S. average at $89.09 a month as of May 2026, covering all sizes and both standard and climate-controlled units. Small units average less and large ones more, so check prices for the size you need near you.

What is the cost of a 10x20 storage unit?

A 10×20 averaged $130.14 a month in SpareFoot’s 2026 year-to-date figures through May, or about $0.65 per square foot. That is the size of a standard one-car garage, which SpareFoot says fits the contents of a three- or four-bedroom home. Local prices, climate control and promotions change what you pay.

What is the cheapest price for a storage unit?

The smallest standard units cost least: SpareFoot’s 2026 year-to-date average for a 5×5 was $34.89 a month through May. By state, Oklahoma had the lowest average at $55.83 a month as of May 2026. Move-in promotions, off-season timing (November to March) and skipping climate control can lower the price.

Can I rent a storage unit for only a month?

In most cases, yes. SpareFoot says self-storage leases are typically month to month. Read the rental agreement for minimum terms, since some promotional rates require a longer commitment. Expect rent increases over time too: SpareFoot says increases every six to 18 months, often 5% to 10%, are not unusual.

Is a 10x10 storage unit enough?

A 10×10 holds about two bedrooms plus a living room, per SpareFoot’s size guide: mattress sets, dressers, a sofa, chairs and shelves. That usually covers a one- or two-bedroom home. For three bedrooms, SpareFoot suggests a 10×15 or 10×20.
