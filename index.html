Where the numbers come from

Every price comparison on ZipCost uses the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis’s Regional Price Parities for 2024, released February 19, 2026, which compare price levels across all 50 states, the District of Columbia and 387 metro areas. The comparisons cover states and metro areas, not individual ZIP codes. The moving calculator’s default fuel price is the U.S. Energy Information Administration’s weekly average for regular gasoline ($4.319 at launch, for the week of September 14, 2026). We refresh the price levels when BEA publishes its yearly update and the fuel price each week after EIA’s release. The guides name the publisher and date of every mover and storage price they quote. The site is maintained by ZipCost. The methodology page shows every formula.

Common questions

What is the 50/20/30 budget rule?

It is the same guideline as 50/30/20, with the numbers listed in a different order: 50% of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. More in Budget Calculator (50/30/20).

What is the 70/20/10 rule money?

It splits take-home pay into 70% for spending, 20% for saving and investing, and 10% for extra debt payments or giving, as Chase describes it. Unlike 50/30/20, it groups needs and wants together. More in Budget Calculator (50/30/20).

What is the best free budget calculator?

The best one is the one you will keep using. Look for a tool that starts from take-home pay, lets you change every percentage, and shows monthly, weekly and yearly amounts so the numbers match how you are paid. More in Budget Calculator (50/30/20).

How do I calculate my cost of living?

Add up what you spend in a typical month on housing, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, debt payments and everything else, then multiply by 12. More in Cost of Living Calculator.

Is $2000 a month a livable wage?

For a single adult, $2,000 a month ($24,000 a year) falls short even in a low-cost state by one widely used measure. More in Cost of Living Calculator.

How much is a 3% cost of living raise?

A 3% raise equals your current salary times 0.03. On $60,000, that is $1,800 more a year, or $150 a month before taxes. For comparison, Social Security benefits rose 2.8% for 2026. More in Cost of Living Calculator.
