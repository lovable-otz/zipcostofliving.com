How Much Do Movers Cost?

Local movers charge about $80 to $100 an hour for a two-person crew, and a move of more than 1,000 miles averages around $5,000, according to ConsumerAffairs (updated May 14, 2026). So how much do movers cost for you? It depends on your home’s size, the distance, and the services you add.

How much do local movers cost?

Local moves are usually billed by the hour. ConsumerAffairs (May 14, 2026) puts a two-person crew at $80 to $100 an hour. HomeGuide (November 5, 2025) quotes $50 to $100 or more per mover per hour, often with a 3- to 5-hour minimum, and $900 to $2,800 to move a 2- or 3-bedroom home less than 50 miles.

Both guides break costs out by home size. Their ranges differ because their crew sizes, hours and data differ, so treat them as two separate reference points rather than one answer.

To turn an hourly rate into a budget, multiply the rate by the hours the company estimates, then add supplies, any fees and a tip. The moving cost calculator adds the rest of the move to your quote.

How much does a long-distance move cost?

Long-distance movers usually charge a flat price based on distance and the weight or volume of your things, not by the hour. ConsumerAffairs (May 14, 2026) puts the average near $5,000 for moves over 1,000 miles and says professional moves over 100 miles typically run $2,000 to $8,000 depending on the load.

HomeGuide (November 5, 2025) gives wider ranges by home size and distance:

Cross-country moves can go higher. U-Pack (2026), which sells moving containers, puts moves of more than 2,500 miles at $5,000 to $25,000 depending on home size, date and the type of service.

What affects a moving quote?

Distance and how much you own set most of the price. After that, access, timing and extra services decide the rest. Stairs, long walks to the truck, extra stops, bulky or specialty items, and peak-season dates all add cost. So do packing services and extra coverage for your belongings.

HomeGuide (November 5, 2025) reports that summer, Friday and weekend moves cost more, as do moves at the start or end of the month. It lists parking permits at $25 to $100 on average and professional packing at $600 to $2,500. ConsumerAffairs (May 14, 2026) puts packing and unpacking at an extra $350 to $600 and lists common add-on charges:

  • Packing materials such as boxes and bubble wrap
  • Stair fees
  • Specialty items such as a piano or fragile artwork
  • A fuel surcharge

To compare quotes fairly, consider asking each company to price the same inventory, dates and services. A quote based on a walk-through or video survey of your home is usually more reliable than one based on a room count. If you need somewhere to keep things between homes, see what a storage unit costs.

Binding vs non-binding estimates

For interstate moves, federal rules define both. Under FMCSA’s household goods regulations, 49 CFR Part 375 (Oct. 1, 2024 edition), a binding estimate guarantees the total cost for the items and services it lists. A non-binding estimate is the mover’s best guess, and your final bill follows the actual weight and the mover’s tariff.

The same rules set what you pay at delivery:

  • Binding: you pay the estimate, plus any services you added after the bill of lading was issued. A mover may charge for writing a binding estimate. If you add goods or services before loading, the mover doesn’t have to honor the original estimate and must settle the change before loading, for example by reaffirming it or writing a new one.
  • Non-binding: the estimate must be free. If you pay up to 110% of it at delivery, the mover must hand over your shipment at delivery.
  • Either type: charges for “impracticable operations” (as defined in the mover’s tariff) collected at delivery can’t exceed 15% of the other charges due.

An interstate mover must give you the estimate in writing before you sign the bill of lading, along with the FMCSA booklet “Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move” or a link to it. These federal rules cover interstate moves; for a move within one state, check your state’s rules.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the average cost for local movers?

Expect roughly $80 to $100 an hour for two movers, per ConsumerAffairs (May 14, 2026), or about $240 to $500 for a one-bedroom home. HomeGuide (November 5, 2025) puts a 2- or 3-bedroom move under 50 miles at $900 to $2,800. Your quote will depend on crew size, hours, stairs and the date.

How much do movers cost for 3 hours?

At the $80 to $100 hourly rate ConsumerAffairs reports for a two-person crew, three hours comes to about $240 to $300 before supplies, fees and a tip. Check the company’s minimum first: HomeGuide notes that a 3- to 5-hour minimum is common, so a short job may be billed as a longer one.

Is $100 enough to tip movers?

Often, yes, for a small local crew. ConsumerAffairs (May 14, 2026) suggests $20 to $30 per crew member for local moves and $50 per crew member for cross-country moves. For two movers on a local job that is $40 to $60; a four-person cross-country crew would be $200. Tipping is optional.

How much should I budget for a move?

Budget for the whole move, not just the movers. Add packing supplies, travel, and the deposit and first month’s rent at the new place. In our moving cost calculator’s default example, a $2,800 movers’ quote grows to $6,500 once a 10% tip, supplies, travel and $3,000 of move-in costs are counted.

Can I rent a storage unit for only a month?

Usually, yes. SpareFoot (May 29, 2026) says self-storage is typically rented month to month, and it put the national average at $89.09 a month as of May 2026. Ask about minimum terms, fees and insurance before you sign, since some promotional rates need a longer commitment.
