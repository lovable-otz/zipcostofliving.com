Budget Calculator (50/30/20)

A budget calculator splits your take-home pay into spending groups. Under the 50/30/20 guideline, which Bankrate (2024) traces to Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi, $5,000 a month becomes $2,500 for needs, $1,500 for wants and $1,000 for savings. You can change each percentage.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By ZipCost

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

The 50/30/20 rule divides after-tax income three ways: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. Elizabeth Warren and her daughter, Amelia Warren Tyagi, described it in their book All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan, as Bankrate explains (August 5, 2024).

The table uses $5,000 of monthly take-home pay. Weekly amounts are the monthly figure × 12 ÷ 52, the same way the calculator shows them.

Treat the split as a starting point rather than a target you must hit. If your own percentages do not add up to 100%, the tool tells you.

What counts as a need vs a want?

A need is a cost you have to pay to live and work: housing, utilities, groceries, transportation, insurance and minimum loan payments. A want is spending you could cut without real harm, such as dining out, subscriptions, travel and hobbies. Savings covers money set aside and debt paid beyond the minimum.

  • Needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, health insurance, getting to work, child care, minimum debt payments.
  • Wants: restaurants and takeout, streaming, gym memberships, vacations, entertainment.
  • Savings: an emergency fund, retirement contributions, extra payments on loans or cards.

Gray areas are normal. A basic phone plan is a need; the newest phone may not be. Groceries are a need; premium brands lean toward a want. Sort each line honestly, because moving wants into the needs column is the quickest way for the split to stop working.

How to adjust 50/30/20 in an expensive city

In a high-cost area, rent alone can take most of the 50% for needs, so the split may need to shift. One option is 60/30/10, which a TIME article (March 15, 2024) quotes a wealth management professor suggesting for young adults with decades until retirement. On $5,000 take-home, that is $3,000, $1,500 and $500.

The housing gap between places is wide. In BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities, rents in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area sit at 194.718, almost twice the U.S. housing level of 100.629, while Monroe, Louisiana sits at 42.815.

Other ways to keep the split working:

  • Take the extra from wants before savings.
  • Share housing; the rent split calculator divides one rent fairly.
  • Check what your pay is worth elsewhere with the cost of living calculator.

If you lower savings for a while, consider raising the share again when your income grows.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 50/20/30 budget rule?

It is the same guideline as 50/30/20, with the numbers listed in a different order: 50% of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. When you read a source that uses 50/20/30, check which category each number belongs to, since the order changes but the shares do not.

What is the 70/20/10 rule money?

It splits take-home pay into 70% for spending, 20% for saving and investing, and 10% for extra debt payments or giving, as Chase describes it. Unlike 50/30/20, it groups needs and wants together. On $5,000 a month, that is $3,500, $1,000 and $500. Enter 70, 10 and 20 in the calculator’s boxes to try it.

What is the best free budget calculator?

The best one is the one you will keep using. Look for a tool that starts from take-home pay, lets you change every percentage, and shows monthly, weekly and yearly amounts so the numbers match how you are paid.
