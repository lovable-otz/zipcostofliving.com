Moving Cost Calculator

A 500-mile truck move with $3,000 of deposit and first rent needs about $4,451 in cash in this moving cost calculator’s default example, with fuel priced at the EIA’s $4.319 U.S. regular gasoline average for the week of September 14, 2026. Swap in your own quotes to see your total.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By ZipCost

How much does it cost to move?

A move costs the transport itself plus everything around it: supplies, travel, and the deposit and first rent at the new place. In this tool’s default truck example, the truck and fuel are about a quarter of the cash you need. The deposit and first month’s rent, $3,000 together, are the biggest line.

Here is that default example line by line: a 500-mile move, a truck at $40 a day for three days plus $0.99 a mile, 10 miles per gallon, and fuel at the EIA weekly average of $4.319.

Renting a truck vs hiring movers

Renting a truck usually costs less in cash, while movers cost more and take the labor off your hands. Switch the tool to “Hire movers” and it replaces the truck, fuel and helpers with the movers’ quote plus a tip. With a $2,800 quote and a 10% tip, the same move needs $6,500 instead of $4,450.95.

That $6,500 breaks down as the $2,800 quote, a $280 tip, the same $150 of supplies, $270 of hotel and food, and $3,000 for the deposit and first rent. The 10% tip is only the tool’s starting value; change it to what you plan to give.

If your movers cross a state line, federal rules apply. Under FMCSA’s household goods regulations (49 CFR 375.401, Oct. 1, 2024 edition), an interstate mover must give you a written estimate before the bill of lading is signed and say whether it is binding or non-binding. A non-binding estimate must be free. The same rules require the mover to give you the FMCSA booklet “Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move,” or a link to it, with the estimate.

For what movers charge before you have a quote, see how much movers cost, where each price is tied to a named, dated source.

Costs people forget when moving

The costs people forget most are the ones at the new home: the security deposit, first month’s rent, utility deposits, pet fees and storage between leases. Travel meals and a hotel night also add up. The tool has a line for each, plus an “anything else” box, so your total covers move day and move-in day.

Some other items to price before you go:

  • Partial rent. If your lease starts mid-month, the first payment may be prorated. The prorated rent calculator works it out three ways.
  • Storage. A gap between leases may mean a month or more of storage. See what a storage unit costs.
  • Fuel for your own car if you drive it alongside the truck.
  • Overlap rent if the old and new leases overlap.

Don’t count on a tax break to cover the bill. IRS Topic 455 (reviewed February 25, 2026) limits the moving expense deduction to active-duty members of the Armed Forces moving under military orders, plus certain intelligence community employees moving in 2026 or later.

Frequently asked questions

How do I estimate the cost of a move?

List every cost and add them up: truck rental and mileage or the movers’ quote, fuel, helpers or a tip, packing supplies, travel, and the deposit and first rent at the new place. This calculator does that sum for you. Use written quotes wherever you can, because published averages rarely match your route, date and load.

How do I calculate average moving cost?

An average won’t tell you what your move costs, so this tool builds the total from your own inputs instead. For a truck, it multiplies the daily rate by rental days, adds the mileage fee, and prices fuel as miles ÷ MPG × fuel price. Everything else is added as entered.

Do interstate movers have to give a written estimate?

Yes. FMCSA rules in 49 CFR 375.401 require an interstate household goods mover to give you a written estimate before you sign the bill of lading, marked binding or non-binding. A mover may charge for a binding estimate but not for a non-binding one. The estimate is usually based on a physical survey of your belongings, which you can waive in writing.
