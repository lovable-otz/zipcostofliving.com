Cost of Living in Mississippi

Prices in Mississippi were 13% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Mississippi = 86.953). Housing stood at 56.456. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $52,172 in Mississippi.

What a salary is worth in Mississippi

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Mississippi sits at 87.0, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 96.2 (3.8% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 56.5 (43.5% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Mississippi it runs 43.5% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Mississippi is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 4 metro areas the BEA publishes for Mississippi, Memphis, TN-MS-AR is the most expensive at 92.2 and Hattiesburg, MS the least at 87.6 — a spread of 4.5 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
