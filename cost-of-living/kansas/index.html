Cost of Living in Kansas

Prices in Kansas were 9.9% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Kansas = 90.068). Housing stood at 71.156. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $54,041 in Kansas.

What a salary is worth in Kansas

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Kansas sits at 90.1, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 94.0 (6.0% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 71.2 (28.8% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Kansas it runs 28.8% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Kansas is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 7 metro areas the BEA publishes for Kansas, Kansas City, MO-KS is the most expensive at 92.5 and Joplin, MO-KS the least at 85.7 — a spread of 6.8 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
