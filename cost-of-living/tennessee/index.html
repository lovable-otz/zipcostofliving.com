Cost of Living in Tennessee

Prices in Tennessee were 8.1% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Tennessee = 91.87). Housing stood at 79.07. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $55,122 in Tennessee.

What makes Tennessee cheaper or more expensive?

Tennessee prices were 8.1% below the U.S. average in BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities (91.87), placing it 38th of 51. Utilities give the largest discount of any category, 27.9% below average (72.137), and rents are 20.9% below (79.07). Goods (96.247) and other services (95.142) are cheaper too, though by less.

The Census Bureau’s 2024 American Community Survey puts the median gross rent at $1,284 a month (U.S.: $1,487) and the median owner-occupied home value at $332,600 (U.S.: $360,600). Rent is about $200 a month under the national median.

Every Tennessee metro scores below 100. Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin is the most expensive at 96.338 and the only metro with housing above average (104.626). Matching a $60,000 salary there takes about $57,803. Jackson is the least expensive at 85.522, needing about $51,313, a gap of $6,490. Knoxville, Memphis and Chattanooga sit together between 91 and 93. Memphis, Chattanooga, Clarksville and Kingsport-Bristol all cross into neighboring states, so their figures include those areas. What should movers to Tennessee budget for?

Tennessee does not tax wages but has the second-highest combined sales tax in the country, so everyday purchases cost more at the register.

  • Income tax: Tennessee is one of eight states with no individual income tax, per the Tax Foundation (as of February 11, 2026).
  • Sales tax: a 7.00% state rate plus a 2.61% average local rate, 9.61% combined, the 2nd highest combined rate in the country as of July 1, 2026, per the Tax Foundation.
  • Security deposit: Tennessee Code section 66-28-301 requires landlords to keep tenants’ security deposits in an account used only for that purpose. A renter guide posted by the Tennessee Department of Health on the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act is written for tenants in 17 counties, including Davidson, Shelby, Knox and Hamilton. It advises getting a receipt for the deposit and notes the landlord must tell you which bank holds it (checked September 16, 2026).
  • Car: the Tennessee Department of Revenue says people moving from another state title and register through their local county clerk. Bring your current out-of-state registration and your lender’s name and address if you have a loan (checked September 16, 2026). County fees vary.

What drives the cost of living in Tennessee

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 96.2 (3.8% below the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 72.1 (27.9% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Tennessee it runs 20.9% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Tennessee is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 10 metro areas the BEA publishes for Tennessee, Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin, TN is the most expensive at 96.3 and Jackson, TN the least at 85.5 — a spread of 10.8 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
