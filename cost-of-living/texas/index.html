Cost of Living in Texas

Prices in Texas were 2.9% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Texas = 97.057). Housing stood at 96.503. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $58,234 in Texas.

What makes Texas cheaper or more expensive?

Texas prices ran 2.9% below the U.S. average in BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities (97.057, rank 25 of 51). Utilities give the biggest discount at 12.5% below average (87.512). Rents (96.503), goods (98.083) and other services (97.081) all sit a few points under 100, so the savings are spread across the budget rather than coming from one line.

The Census Bureau’s 2024 American Community Survey puts the median gross rent at $1,475 a month (U.S.: $1,487) and the median owner-occupied home value at $313,200 (U.S.: $360,600). Rent is roughly level with the nation; home values are lower.

The state average hides a wide range. Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is the priciest Texas metro at 103.09, the only one above the U.S. average. Eagle Pass is the cheapest wholly Texan metro at 83.805. Matching a $60,000 salary takes about $61,854 in Dallas-Fort Worth versus $50,283 in Eagle Pass, a gap of $11,571. Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos has the highest housing figure in the state (120.361) yet an overall index of 98.066, because its other prices are closer to average. Border metros such as McAllen, Brownsville and Laredo sit near 86 to 87. Compare two specific places with the cost of living calculator.

What should movers to Texas budget for?

Texas has no tax on wages, but sales tax, vehicle fees and a new-resident vehicle tax add to the cost of arriving.

  • Income tax: Texas is one of eight states with no individual income tax, per the Tax Foundation (as of February 11, 2026).
  • Sales tax: a 6.25% state rate plus a 1.95% average local rate, 8.20% combined, the 14th highest combined rate in the country as of July 1, 2026, per the Tax Foundation.
  • Security deposit: Texas Property Code section 92.103 requires the landlord to refund the deposit on or before the 30th day after you surrender the unit.
  • Car: TxDMV gives new residents 30 days to register. The state base registration fee is $50.75 plus $1 for TexasSure, and counties may add fees. New residents also owe a new-resident tax of $90, or the difference between the sales tax paid in the previous state and the Texas rate. Emissions testing applies in 17 counties, including Dallas, Harris, Tarrant and Travis (checked September 16, 2026).

What drives the cost of living in Texas

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 98.1 (1.9% below the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 87.5 (12.5% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Texas it runs 3.5% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is not the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

How Texas compares with other states

Texas ranks 25 of 51 for overall prices, where 1 is the most expensive. 24 states and territories in the series are more expensive and 26 are cheaper. A move changes your costs by the gap between two RPPs, not by either figure on its own — use the cost of living calculator to compare Texas against the place you are considering, and the salary comparison tool to see what a given offer is worth in each.

Where in Texas is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 26 metro areas the BEA publishes for Texas, Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington, TX is the most expensive at 103.1 and Eagle Pass, TX the least at 83.8 — a spread of 19.3 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
