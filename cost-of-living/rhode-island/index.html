Cost of Living in Rhode Island

Prices in Rhode Island were 2.3% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Rhode Island = 102.28). Housing stood at 105.62. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $61,368 in Rhode Island.

What a salary is worth in Rhode Island

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Rhode Island sits at 102.3, so a dollar buys less here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Rhode Island

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 146.7 (46.7% above the national level). goods pulls hardest the other way at 97.2 (2.8% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Rhode Island it runs 5.6% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is not the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.
