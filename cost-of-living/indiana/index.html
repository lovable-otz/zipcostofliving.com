Cost of Living in Indiana

Prices in Indiana were 6.7% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Indiana = 93.329). Housing stood at 73.926. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $55,997 in Indiana.

What a salary is worth in Indiana

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Indiana sits at 93.3, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. What drives the cost of living in Indiana

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 99.1 (0.9% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 73.9 (26.1% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Indiana it runs 26.1% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Indiana is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 15 metro areas the BEA publishes for Indiana, Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN is the most expensive at 103.6 and Terre Haute, IN the least at 87.8 — a spread of 15.8 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
