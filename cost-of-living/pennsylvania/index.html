Cost of Living in Pennsylvania

Prices in Pennsylvania were 2.4% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Pennsylvania = 97.572). Housing stood at 85.068. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $58,543 in Pennsylvania.

What a salary is worth in Pennsylvania

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Pennsylvania sits at 97.6, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 109.3 (9.3% above the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 85.1 (14.9% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Pennsylvania it runs 14.9% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Pennsylvania is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 16 metro areas the BEA publishes for Pennsylvania, Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington, PA-NJ-DE-MD is the most expensive at 102.6 and Johnstown, PA the least at 85.9 — a spread of 16.6 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
