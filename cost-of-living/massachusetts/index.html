Cost of Living in Massachusetts

Prices in Massachusetts were 5.8% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Massachusetts = 105.757). Housing stood at 128.068. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $63,454 in Massachusetts.

What a salary is worth in Massachusetts

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Massachusetts sits at 105.8, so a dollar buys less here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 152.1 (52.1% above the national level). goods pulls hardest the other way at 98.8 (1.2% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Massachusetts it runs 28.1% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Massachusetts is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 7 metro areas the BEA publishes for Massachusetts, Boston-Cambridge-Newton, MA-NH is the most expensive at 108.3 and Pittsfield, MA the least at 95.1 — a spread of 13.2 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
