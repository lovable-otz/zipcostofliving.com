Cost of Living in Idaho

Prices in Idaho were 4.5% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Idaho = 95.494). Housing stood at 90.027. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $57,296 in Idaho.

What a salary is worth in Idaho

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Idaho sits at 95.5, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 99.0 (1.0% below the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 70.4 (29.6% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Idaho it runs 10.0% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Idaho is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 6 metro areas the BEA publishes for Idaho, Boise City, ID is the most expensive at 98.4 and Pocatello, ID the least at 88.9 — a spread of 9.5 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
