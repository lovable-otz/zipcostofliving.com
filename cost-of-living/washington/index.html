Cost of Living in Washington

Prices in Washington were 7% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Washington = 107.013). Housing stood at 125.979. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $64,208 in Washington.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

Washington prices were 7% above the U.S. average in BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities (107.013), the 6th most expensive of 51. Rents are the main driver at 26% above average (125.979). Goods (104.359) and other services (103.873) add a smaller premium, while utilities are 7.1% cheaper than average (92.86).

The Census Bureau’s 2024 American Community Survey puts the median gross rent at $1,824 a month (U.S.: $1,487) and the median owner-occupied home value at $602,200 (U.S.: $360,600). Home values are about two-thirds higher than the national median.

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue leads the state at 111.133, with housing at 151.314. Matching a $60,000 salary there takes about $66,680. The cheapest metro entirely inside Washington is Yakima at 95.545, needing about $57,327, a gap of $9,353. Lewiston scores lower (91.16), but that metro is mostly in Idaho. Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro (105.421) also crosses the state line, so its figure blends Oregon and Washington prices. East of the Cascades, Spokane-Spokane Valley and Kennewick-Richland both sit almost exactly at the national average. The cost of living calculator compares any two of these areas.

What should movers to Washington budget for?

Washington does not tax wages today, but it has one of the highest sales taxes and a new tax on very high incomes starting in 2028.

  • Income tax: the Tax Foundation (as of February 11, 2026) lists Washington as taxing capital gains only, at 7%, and 9% above $1 million. The Washington Department of Revenue says a new 9.9% tax on adjusted gross income above $1 million takes effect January 1, 2028, with first returns due in April 2029. SB 6346 was signed on March 30, 2026.
  • Sales tax: a 6.50% state rate plus a 3.07% average local rate, 9.57% combined, the 3rd highest combined rate in the country as of July 1, 2026, per the Tax Foundation.
  • Security deposit: RCW 59.18.260 lets a landlord collect a deposit only with a written lease and a signed checklist of the unit’s condition, and bars deductions for normal wear. Under RCW 59.18.280, the landlord has 30 days after you move out to refund the deposit or explain what was kept.
  • Car: the Department of Licensing gives new residents 30 days to get a Washington driver license and register their vehicles, and the license must come first (checked September 16, 2026).

What drives the cost of living in Washington

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Housing is the biggest upward pull at 126.0 (26.0% above the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 92.9 (7.1% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Washington it runs 26.0% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Washington is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 13 metro areas the BEA publishes for Washington, Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue, WA is the most expensive at 111.1 and Lewiston, ID-WA the least at 91.2 — a spread of 20.0 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
