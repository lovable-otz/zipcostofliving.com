Cost of Living in Maryland

Prices in Maryland were 5% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Maryland = 104.959). Housing stood at 121.079. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $62,975 in Maryland.

What a salary is worth in Maryland

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Maryland sits at 105.0, so a dollar buys less here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Maryland

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Housing is the biggest upward pull at 121.1 (21.1% above the national level). services pulls hardest the other way at 101.6 (1.6% above). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Maryland it runs 21.1% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Maryland is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 6 metro areas the BEA publishes for Maryland, Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV is the most expensive at 108.9 and Hagerstown-Martinsburg, MD-WV the least at 94.2 — a spread of 14.7 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
