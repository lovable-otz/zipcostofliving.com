Cost of Living in Ohio

Prices in Ohio were 7.2% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Ohio = 92.774). Housing stood at 73.012. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $55,664 in Ohio.

What a salary is worth in Ohio

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Ohio sits at 92.8, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Ohio

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 98.9 (1.1% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 73.0 (27.0% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Ohio it runs 27.0% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

How Ohio compares with other states

Ohio ranks 35 of 51 for overall prices, where 1 is the most expensive. 34 states and territories in the series are more expensive and 16 are cheaper. Where in Ohio is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 15 metro areas the BEA publishes for Ohio, Columbus, OH is the most expensive at 95.5 and Youngstown-Warren, OH the least at 87.4 — a spread of 8.1 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
