Cost of Living in Wisconsin

Prices in Wisconsin were 5.9% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Wisconsin = 94.095). Housing stood at 79.312. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $56,457 in Wisconsin.

What a salary is worth in Wisconsin

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Wisconsin sits at 94.1, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Wisconsin

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 99.5 (0.5% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 79.3 (20.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Wisconsin it runs 20.7% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Wisconsin is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 15 metro areas the BEA publishes for Wisconsin, Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington, MN-WI is the most expensive at 104.8 and Duluth, MN-WI the least at 88.8 — a spread of 16.1 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
