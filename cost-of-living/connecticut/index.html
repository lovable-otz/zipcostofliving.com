Cost of Living in Connecticut

Prices in Connecticut were 3.6% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Connecticut = 103.61). Housing stood at 117.048. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $62,166 in Connecticut.

What a salary is worth in Connecticut

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Connecticut sits at 103.6, so a dollar buys less here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 146.5 (46.5% above the national level). goods pulls hardest the other way at 97.3 (2.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Connecticut it runs 17.0% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Connecticut is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 4 metro areas the BEA publishes for Connecticut, New Haven, CT is the most expensive at 104.6 and Waterbury-Shelton, CT the least at 99.8 — a spread of 4.8 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
