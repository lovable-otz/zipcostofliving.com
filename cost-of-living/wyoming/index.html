Cost of Living in Wyoming

Prices in Wyoming were 7.3% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Wyoming = 92.691). Housing stood at 71.127. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $55,615 in Wyoming.

What a salary is worth in Wyoming

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Wyoming sits at 92.7, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 100.4 (0.4% above the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 71.1 (28.9% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Wyoming it runs 28.9% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Wyoming is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 2 metro areas the BEA publishes for Wyoming, Cheyenne, WY is the most expensive at 96.5 and Casper, WY the least at 93.8 — a spread of 2.7 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
