Cost of Living in South Carolina

Prices in South Carolina were 6.3% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; South Carolina = 93.749). Housing stood at 80.49. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $56,249 in South Carolina.

What a salary is worth in South Carolina

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. South Carolina sits at 93.7, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in South Carolina

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 98.3 (1.7% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 80.5 (19.5% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in South Carolina it runs 19.5% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in South Carolina is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 10 metro areas the BEA publishes for South Carolina, Charleston-North Charleston, SC is the most expensive at 101.0 and Florence, SC the least at 86.8 — a spread of 14.2 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
