Cost of Living in Maine

Prices in Maine were 3% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Maine = 97.05). Housing stood at 78.94. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $58,230 in Maine.

What a salary is worth in Maine

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Maine sits at 97.0, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Maine

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 135.2 (35.2% above the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 78.9 (21.1% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Maine it runs 21.1% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Maine ranks 26 of 51 for overall prices, where 1 is the most expensive. 25 states and territories in the series are more expensive and 25 are cheaper. Where in Maine is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 3 metro areas the BEA publishes for Maine, Portland-South Portland, ME is the most expensive at 101.9 and Lewiston-Auburn, ME the least at 94.7 — a spread of 7.1 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
