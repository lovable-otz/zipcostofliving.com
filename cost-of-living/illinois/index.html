Cost of Living in Illinois

Prices in Illinois were same as U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Illinois = 99.958). Housing stood at 93.944. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $59,975 in Illinois.

What a salary is worth in Illinois

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Illinois sits at 100.0, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Illinois

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 103.8 (3.8% above the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 85.0 (15.0% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Illinois it runs 6.1% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Illinois is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 12 metro areas the BEA publishes for Illinois, Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN is the most expensive at 103.6 and Cape Girardeau, MO-IL the least at 86.1 — a spread of 17.5 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
