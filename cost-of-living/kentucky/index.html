Cost of Living in Kentucky

Prices in Kentucky were 9.8% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Kentucky = 90.159). Housing stood at 64.313. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $54,095 in Kentucky.

What a salary is worth in Kentucky

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Kentucky sits at 90.2, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 97.2 (2.8% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 64.3 (35.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Kentucky it runs 35.7% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Kentucky is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 9 metro areas the BEA publishes for Kentucky, Cincinnati, OH-KY-IN is the most expensive at 95.4 and Paducah, KY-IL the least at 86.1 — a spread of 9.3 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
