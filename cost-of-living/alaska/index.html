Cost of Living in Alaska

Prices in Alaska were 2.4% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Alaska = 102.359). Housing stood at 93.784. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $61,415 in Alaska.

What a salary is worth in Alaska

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Alaska sits at 102.4, so a dollar buys less here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 119.0 (19.0% above the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 93.8 (6.2% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Alaska it runs 6.2% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Alaska is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 2 metro areas the BEA publishes for Alaska, Anchorage, AK is the most expensive at 105.4 and Fairbanks-College, AK the least at 103.2 — a spread of 2.2 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
