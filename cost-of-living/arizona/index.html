Cost of Living in Arizona

Prices in Arizona were 0.7% above U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Arizona = 100.677). Housing stood at 106.804. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $60,406 in Arizona.

What makes Arizona cheaper or more expensive?

Arizona prices were just 0.7% above the U.S. average in BEA’s 2024 Regional Price Parities (100.677, rank 17 of 51). Rents (106.804) and other services (102.808) run above average. Goods (95.361) and utilities (92.317) are cheaper, and the two sides nearly cancel out.

The Census Bureau’s 2024 American Community Survey puts the median gross rent at $1,672 a month (U.S.: $1,487) and the median owner-occupied home value at $426,000 (U.S.: $360,600). Both sit above the national figures, consistent with the higher housing index.

Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is the most expensive metro at 103.316, with housing at 121.236, and it holds most of the state’s population. Matching a $60,000 salary there takes about $61,990. Sierra Vista-Douglas, in the southeast corner, is the least expensive at 89.243, needing about $53,546, a gap of $8,444. Flagstaff (100.325) sits at the national average despite being a smaller city, and Tucson (96.896) comes in almost six and a half points below Phoenix. Yuma and Lake Havasu City-Kingman are both under 95. The cost of living calculator shows the salary gap between any two of these metros.

What should movers to Arizona budget for?

Arizona taxes wages at a low flat rate, has an above-average combined sales tax, and caps the deposit a landlord can require.

  • Income tax: a flat 2.50%, per the Tax Foundation (data as of February 11, 2026).
  • Sales tax: a 5.60% state rate plus a 2.94% average local rate, 8.54% combined, the 11th highest combined rate in the country as of July 1, 2026, per the Tax Foundation. Cities and counties add their own rates, so the total varies by address.
  • Security deposit: Arizona Revised Statutes section 33-1321 limits security, including prepaid rent, to one and one-half months’ rent. On a $1,672 rent, the state median, that caps the deposit at $2,508. After you move out and ask for your deposit, the landlord has 14 days, not counting weekends or legal holidays, to send an itemized list of deductions and the balance due.

Because the deposit and first month’s rent can both be due at signing, set aside roughly two and a half months’ rent in cash before you arrive. Check the monthly side with the rent affordability calculator, and the one-time side with the moving cost calculator.

What drives the cost of living in Arizona

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Housing is the biggest upward pull at 106.8 (6.8% above the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 92.3 (7.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Arizona it runs 6.8% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Arizona is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 7 metro areas the BEA publishes for Arizona, Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler, AZ is the most expensive at 103.3 and Sierra Vista-Douglas, AZ the least at 89.2 — a spread of 14.1 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
