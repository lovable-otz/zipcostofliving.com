Cost of Living in Delaware

Prices in Delaware were 0.2% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Delaware = 99.808). Housing stood at 102.015. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $59,885 in Delaware.

What a salary is worth in Delaware

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Delaware sits at 99.8, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Utilities is the biggest upward pull at 103.8 (3.8% above the national level). goods pulls hardest the other way at 96.2 (3.8% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Delaware it runs 2.0% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is not the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Delaware is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 2 metro areas the BEA publishes for Delaware, Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington, PA-NJ-DE-MD is the most expensive at 102.6 and Dover, DE the least at 97.5 — a spread of 5.0 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
