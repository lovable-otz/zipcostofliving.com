Cost of Living in Alabama

Prices in Alabama were 11.2% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Alabama = 88.823). Housing stood at 61.785. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $53,294 in Alabama.

What a salary is worth in Alabama

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Alabama sits at 88.8, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Alabama

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 96.7 (3.3% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 61.8 (38.2% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Alabama it runs 38.2% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Alabama is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 13 metro areas the BEA publishes for Alabama, Daphne-Fairhope-Foley, AL is the most expensive at 94.7 and Dothan, AL the least at 83.8 — a spread of 10.9 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
