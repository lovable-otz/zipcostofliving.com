Cost of Living in Iowa

Prices in Iowa were 12.2% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Iowa = 87.762). Housing stood at 65.322. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $52,657 in Iowa.

What a salary is worth in Iowa

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Iowa sits at 87.8, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

What drives the cost of living in Iowa

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Goods is the biggest upward pull at 93.7 (6.3% below the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 65.3 (34.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Iowa it runs 34.7% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

How Iowa compares with other states

Iowa ranks 49 of 51 for overall prices, where 1 is the most expensive. 48 states and territories in the series are more expensive and 2 are cheaper. A move changes your costs by the gap between two RPPs, not by either figure on its own — use the cost of living calculator to compare Iowa against the place you are considering, and the salary comparison tool to see what a given offer is worth in each.

Where in Iowa is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 9 metro areas the BEA publishes for Iowa, Omaha, NE-IA is the most expensive at 91.9 and Sioux City, IA-NE-SD the least at 86.5 — a spread of 5.5 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
