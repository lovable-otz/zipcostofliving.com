Cost of Living in Nevada

Prices in Nevada were same as U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Nevada = 99.979). Housing stood at 114.135. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $59,987 in Nevada.

What a salary is worth in Nevada

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Nevada sits at 100.0, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. What drives the cost of living in Nevada

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Housing is the biggest upward pull at 114.1 (14.1% above the national level). utilities pulls hardest the other way at 90.5 (9.5% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Nevada it runs 14.1% above the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Nevada is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 3 metro areas the BEA publishes for Nevada, Reno, NV is the most expensive at 101.0 and Carson City, NV the least at 98.1 — a spread of 2.9 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
