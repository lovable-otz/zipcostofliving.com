Cost of Living in Michigan

Prices in Michigan were 3.8% below U.S. average in 2024, by the BEA Regional Price Parities (U.S. = 100; Michigan = 96.217). Housing stood at 82.332. To match a $60,000 salary at average U.S. prices, you would need about $57,730 in Michigan.

What a salary is worth in Michigan

Regional Price Parities restate local prices against a national average of 100. Michigan sits at 96.2, so a dollar buys more here than it does nationally. To hold the same standard of living you would need roughly the figures below — the third column is the difference against the same salary spent at average U.S. prices.

BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 (U.S. = 100)

The headline number is an average of several categories, and they rarely move together. Services is the biggest upward pull at 100.9 (0.9% above the national level). housing pulls hardest the other way at 82.3 (17.7% below). Housing is the category that moves a household budget most, and in Michigan it runs 17.7% below the U.S. level, so rent or mortgage is the main reason the overall figure sits where it does.

Because the categories are weighted by how much a typical household spends, a large gap in a small category moves the overall figure less than a modest gap in housing. That is why two states with the same headline RPP can feel quite different to live in.

Where in Michigan is cheapest?

State averages hide a lot. Among the 16 metro areas the BEA publishes for Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI is the most expensive at 100.9 and Battle Creek, MI the least at 90.5 — a spread of 10.3 points. Rural areas outside these metros are typically cheaper still for housing, though utilities and fuel can offset part of that.
